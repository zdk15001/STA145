      About the data
      
      
        
      All examples come from the first wave (2017) of How Couples Meet and Stay Together (HCMST 2017–2022 public data, version 2.2, produced by Michael Rosenfeld at Stanford). It surveyed 3,510 US adults about their current or most recent partner, and it purposely includes extra lesbian, gay, and bisexual respondents.
      
        
      The tests here are unweighted, like the t.test() you run in class. A published analysis would use the survey weights, so treat these numbers as practice, not as estimates for the whole US population.
      
        
      Variables used:
      
        
        
          
      • rel_quality: “How would you describe the quality of your relationship with [partner]?” Flipped so 5 = excellent, 4 = good, 3 = fair, 2 = poor, 1 = very poor. Asked of people currently in a relationship.
        • 
          
      • how_met: whether they met their partner online, based on how they described meeting and whether they used an internet site or app.
        • 
          
      • marital: married vs. in an unmarried relationship (people currently partnered).
        • 
          
      • living: whether they live with their partner.
        • 
          
      • couple_type: same-sex vs. different-sex couple.
        • 
          
      • met_friends: whether friends of either partner introduced them.
        • 
          
      • age_met: age when they first met their partner.
        • 
          
      • years_to_couple: years from first meeting to the start of the romantic relationship.
        • 
          
      • years_to_movein: years from the start of the relationship to first living together.
        • 
          
      • age_gap: absolute difference between the respondent's age and their partner's age.
        • 
          
      • app_use: among people who met someone new for dating in the past year, whether they used a phone app to do it.
        • 
          
      • rel_years: years since the relationship started (as of July 2017).
