# Room for Error: what it saves and who can see it

*A notice for students. The same information is inside the game under "About, privacy and accessibility".*

Room for Error is a practice game for STA 145, made by Professor Kline with the help of Claude AI, an AI model from Anthropic. **Many errors are present.** The questions were written with AI and have not all been checked, so tell Professor Kline if an answer looks wrong. It is a course tool, not an official website of The College of New Jersey.

**You can get full credit for the assignment without creating an account.**

## Three ways to play

- **Anonymously.** No username. Your scores and decorations are saved only in your browser, on the device you are using. Nothing is sent anywhere.
- **With a username.** Your username, best score on each level, number of tries, and decoration choices are also saved in a Google Sheet that Professor Kline controls. Professor Kline approves every username before anyone else can see it.
- **Logging in.** Your username and your login code bring your progress to any device.

## Usernames are public

Once your username is approved, anyone with the class code can see your username, level, number of perfect scores, and room on the leaderboard, and your username on the login page. **Do not put your name, or anything else that identifies you, in a username.**

## Your login code

When you create a username you are given a short login code. Write it down. You need it, with your username, to log in again, and you cannot log in again until your username is approved. The code is short and easy to guess. It keeps mix-ups out; it is not strong security, so treat this as a game account. If you lose your code, ask Professor Kline for a new one.

## What is never collected

The game never asks for your real name, email address, student ID or a password of your own. It has no ads, no analytics and no tracking cookies. Like any website, the service that delivers the page (GitHub Pages) receives your device's internet address when the page loads, and Google does when the leaderboard is used. The game never sees or stores it.

## Your choices

- You can delete your account at any time from your account (top right). Your progress stays on your device and you keep playing anonymously.
- On a shared or public computer, open your account when you finish and log out or erase your progress from that device.
- Professor Kline can rename or remove a username, and deletes every account after the semester ends.

## Accessibility

The game works with a keyboard, a screen reader, browser zoom and dark mode, and every chart has a text description. If any part of it does not work for you, message Professor Kline through Canvas to get the same practice in another format.
